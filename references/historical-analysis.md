# Historical Analysis Reference

Use this reference for complex person/event/reform/comparison questions.

## Evidence and source location

For a decisive fact, disputed point, or quotation, record the source work and the most precise available locator: volume/chapter, historical year, passage, and edition or URL if relevant. Cite the locator near the claim in the answer or canonical note. A work title alone is insufficient to recheck a causal argument.

If the user provides only an excerpt, cite its supplied location if present. If no location can be verified, say so; keep the claim provisional rather than inventing a volume or wording. Mark a reconstructed motive, coalition, or causal mechanism as inference and identify the facts on which it rests. When sources disagree, keep each source's claim and locator separate.

## 1. Actor Map

For each actor or group:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Goal | What outcome do they actually want? |
| Resources | Army, land, office, money, information, prestige, ruler trust? |
| Incentive | What makes them support or oppose? |
| Constraint | Law, lineage, nobles, finance, military, geography, legitimacy? |
| Risk | What do they most fear losing? |

Focus on whether interests remain aligned over time.

## 2. Interest Map

```text
政策/改革
  ├─ 获益者
  ├─ 受损者
  ├─ 中立者
  ├─ 关键摇摆者
  └─ 有否决能力者
```

Ask whether the reform creates a durable constituency that benefits from its continuation.

## 3. Decision Reconstruction

```text
目标
↓
可见信息
↓
现实选项
↓
收益 / 成本 / 风险
↓
实际选择
↓
短期结果
↓
长期副作用
```

When the user asks “what should he have done?”, produce only period-feasible alternatives and state their costs.

## 4. Causal Layers

Separate:

- structural condition;
- direct cause;
- trigger;
- accelerator;
- feedback loop;
- terminal outcome.

Example:

```text
改革长期依赖君主个人授权
    ↓
既得利益受损但仍保持组织能力
    ↓
改革未形成足够稳定的新支持联盟
    ↓
君主死亡
    ↓
政治保护消失
    ↓
反对派迅速反扑
```

## 5. Personal Capacity vs Institutional Capacity

### Personal

- special ruler support;
- exceptional executor ability;
- prestige/network/coercive capacity;
- ad hoc command.

### Institutional

- stable rules;
- bureaucratic routines;
- fiscal or military self-maintenance;
- durable beneficiary groups;
- successor incentives to preserve the system;
- resilience after leadership turnover.

## 6. Power and Coalition Analysis

Check:

- formal authority;
- actual implementation capacity;
- veto players;
- control of scarce resources;
- coalition alternatives;
- distribution of benefits inside the coalition;
- whether coercion is substituting for consent;
- whether one decision gives former neutrals a shared reason to coordinate.

A recurring mechanism:

> A ruler or leader can increase immediate control while simultaneously making previously separate opponents more willing to coordinate.

## 7. Sima Guang Analysis

For “why is this story here?” inspect:

1. placement in surrounding chronology;
2. nearby cases that form contrast;
3. repeated behaviors emphasized by the narration;
4. explicit comments (`臣光曰` etc.);
5. recurring political propositions: 名分、礼、继承、用人、纳谏、权力边界、战争审慎、制度稳定.

Reserve “Sima Guang's explicit judgment” for a directly attributable statement with a verifiable passage. Selection, emphasis, and sequence support an inference about editorial purpose, not a direct quotation or explicit judgment.

## 9. Mechanism Abstraction

A useful mechanism must include:

- the sequence;
- the actors/incentives involved;
- the conditions under which it works;
- conditions under which it fails;
- at least one comparable case if available.

Do not create a `concept` note from a one-off observation that is unlikely to recur.
